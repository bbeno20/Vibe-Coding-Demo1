# Show & Swap Notes

> Module 2 · Validation. Put the prototype in front of a peer. Their reaction is the data.

## What I showed

https://first-value-flight.lovable.app

## What my partner understood (in their words)

New customers churn because they don’t experience meaningful value early enough and adoption stays with one person. Rather than leaving me to explore the product, you guide me through the shortest path to collaborative value. The key bet is that getting another person involved during week one is a strong enough activation signal to predict better 90-day retention. The Signals screen makes that hypothesis explicit and tests activation against a 40% threshold versus a 22% baseline.

## Where they got confused / pushed back

My biggest pushback is that inviting someone is being treated as the aha moment, but an invitation itself isn’t necessarily value. I could invite a colleague who never accepts or uses the product. ‘Run it together’ actually feels closer to demonstrated collaborative value. I’d also challenge the leap from activation above 40% to ‘cuts 90-day churn’: the prototype establishes a plausible correlation, but doesn’t yet prove that the guided path causes better retention. The three churn quotes strengthen the problem evidence, but they don’t establish causality.

## What I'll change as a result

Tighten the definition of the aha action from invitation to demonstrated collaborative use, e.g. teammate accepts -> engages -> first workflow is run together. I’ll make the causal chain measurable as guided path -> collaborative activation -> repeat usage -> 90-day retention, rather than treating activation alone as proof of retention. I’ll also distinguish the immediate experiment success criterion (>40% activation) from the longer-term business outcome (lower 90-day churn).
